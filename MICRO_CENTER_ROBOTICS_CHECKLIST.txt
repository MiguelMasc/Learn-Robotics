ROBOTICS SHOPPING / BROWSING CHECKLIST
Bay Area - researched July 12, 2026

This list is based on the projects in this repo:
- Programmable differential-drive RC car with safe wireless teleop
- Line following, encoders, odometry, telemetry, and PID control
- Arduino/Pico/ESP32-to-ROS 2 base bridge
- ROS 2 sensor dashboard
- 2D LiDAR SLAM rover and autonomous room mapper
- Later: mobile manipulator and embodied assistant

MAIN RECOMMENDATION
===================

Build in this order:
1. Reliable differential-drive base
2. Wheel encoders + battery telemetry
3. PID speed control and repeatable odometry
4. ROS 2 bridge and high-level computer
5. LiDAR and SLAM

Do not let a good-looking LiDAR, camera, AI HAT, or complete robot kit jump ahead of the base. The repo's own plan correctly says manual control first, odometry second, and SLAM third.

Given the equipment already on hand, the main gap is not another controller or computer. It is a well-specified rolling base, encoder feedback, safe motor power, and the small hardware needed to assemble and test it. The Pololu Romi route below is the best overall match I found.

HIGH PRIORITY - CHECK / PROBABLY BUY
-----------------------------------

[ ] Adafruit DRV8833 dual motor-driver breakout - $5.99 seen
    Why: Compact two-channel H-bridge for SMALL low-voltage motors; useful for a one-motor bench test and a light 2WD car.
    Check before buying: motor voltage and measured/declared stall current. Its motor supply range is 2.7-10.8 V and it does not give comfortable current headroom for every TT motor.
    Important: The Hiwonder encoder TT motor below is specified at 1.2 A stall, so a DRV8833-class board is near its limit rather than generously oversized. A driver with more per-channel headroom is better for jam/reversal tests.
    Link: https://www.microcenter.com/search/search_results.aspx?fq=category%3AMaker%2FSTEM%7C712%2Cbrand%3AAdafruit+Industries

[ ] Klein MM325 multimeter - $34.99, limited stock seen
    Why: Voltage, continuity, resistance, and DC-current measurements are essential for battery sag, brownouts, wiring faults, and motor-current checks.
    In-store test: Make sure the current-input fuse is replaceable and the leads/probe tips are comfortable around small headers.
    Alternative budget meter seen: Eclipse MT-1236 True-RMS, $29.99.
    Link: https://www.microcenter.com/search/search_results.aspx?fq=Category%3AMultiMeters%60+Test+Equipment%7C541&storeid=195

[ ] Basic prototyping consumables
    Look for: heat shrink; screw terminals; JST/locking connectors; zip ties; ferrules; M2/M3 standoffs and screws.
    Do not use loose Dupont jumpers for final motor-power or battery wiring.
    Link: https://www.microcenter.com/search/search_results.aspx?fq=category%3AElectronic+Supplies%7C729&storeid=195

[ ] Power-safety small parts
    Look for: a real main switch, inline fuse and spare fuses, strain relief, 470 uF or larger electrolytic capacitors with suitable voltage rating,
    and 0.1 uF ceramic decoupling capacitors. Buy only after confirming voltage/current ratings and connector polarity.
    Link: https://www.microcenter.com/search/search_results.aspx?fq=category%3AElectronic+Components%7C735&storeid=195


GOOD TO INSPECT - BUY ONLY FOR THE RIGHT MILESTONE
--------------------------------------------------

[ ] Orbbec Oradar MS200 2D LiDAR - $99.99; 4 listed in stock
    Why: 360-degree 2D ranging with ROS/ROS 2 support at a plausible starter price.
    Buy only after: Repeatable encoder odometry and rigid sensor mounting exist.
    Check in store / online before purchase: exact MS200 vs MS200K model, included USB/serial adapter and cable, voltage/current, ROS 2 package support 
    for your Ubuntu/ROS distribution, mounting-hole pattern, return policy, and whether replacement cables are available.
    Comparison target: Slamtec RPLIDAR C1 at about $99.99. Slamtec documents a 12 m radius, 5 kHz sampling, 110 g weight, Class 1 laser, 
    and official ROS/ROS 2 packages.
    Micro Center link: https://www.microcenter.com/search/search_results.aspx?fq=category%3AElectronic+Parts%7C689&storeid=195
    Slamtec product info: https://www.slamtec.com/en/c1

[ ] Adafruit MPU-6050 6-DoF IMU - $12.99; very limited stock seen
    Why: Useful later for comparing inertial readings to encoder odometry and for a balancing/PID rig.
    Do not expect: A low-cost gyro to replace wheel encoders or fix bad mechanics.
    Link: https://www.microcenter.com/search/search_results.aspx?fq=brand%3AAdafruit+Industries%2Ccategory%3ABoards%2FProjects%7C734&storeid=195

[ ] 30 V / 5 A current-limited bench supply - about $109.99-$159.99
    Why: Safely bring up one motor channel, cap current, reproduce brownouts, and measure load response without repeatedly draining batteries.
    Seen: SRA KD3005D around $109.99; KA3005D around $136.99.
    Buy only if: You expect regular electronics work. Maker Nexus may be cheaper for occasional use.
    Link: https://www.microcenter.com/search/search_results.aspx?fq=Category%3AMultiMeters%60+Test+Equipment%7C541&storeid=195

[ ] Matched rechargeable NiMH cells and smart charger
    Why: The repo wisely recommends NiMH for early indoor testing. They are more forgiving than a beginner-built lithium pack.
    For a 4xAA base: Buy four identical cells plus spares.
    For Pololu Romi: Buy six identical cells plus matched spares.
    Do not mix brands, ages, or capacities in one pack. Avoid rectangular 9 V batteries for drive motors.
    Micro Center examples seen: Duracell charger + four 1300 mAh AA cells, $19.99; four 2400 mAh AA cells, $15.99. The mixed capacities make these two listings a poor combined six-cell Romi set, so look for 6-8 identical cells and an independent-channel smart charger.
    Link: https://www.microcenter.com/search/search_results.aspx?fq=Category%3AChargers%60+Rechargeable+Batteries%7C551&storeid=195

[ ] Bambu Lab A1 or A1 mini 3D printer
    A1 mini: $209.99 sale, 180 x 180 x 180 mm, 8 listed in stock.
    A1: $279.99 sale, 256 x 256 x 256 mm, listed in stock.
    Recommendation: If buying for robot plates, sensor masts, brackets, and later manipulator parts, the A1's larger bed is worth the extra $70. 
    The mini is good for small brackets but can force chassis plates into multiple pieces.
    Skip AMS/multicolor initially; it does not make a robot function better.
    First consider one month at Maker Nexus or a library printing service so you learn what you actually need.
    Link: https://www.microcenter.com/search/search_results.aspx?fq=category%3A3D+Printers%7C701&storeID=195


LOOK AT, BUT PROBABLY SKIP
--------------------------

[ ] Hiwonder TT gearmotor with Hall encoder - $27.99 EACH at Micro Center
    Firm recommendation: Do not buy at this price.
    Hiwonder's own current listing showed the same 3-12 V, 150 RPM, 1:42, 1.2 A-stall encoder motor for $6.99. Micro Center is about 4x the direct price before shipping.
    Direct link: https://www.hiwonder.com/products/tt-motor-plastic

[ ] Hiwonder ROS2 Robot Car with mecanum wheels - $129.99
    Why it is tempting: Fast path to something that moves.
    Why it is not the best first base here: Mecanum wheels add lateral slip and more complicated odometry, while the repo explicitly recommends simple differential drive for the first SLAM platform. Inspect its construction for ideas, but do not let the ROS2 label decide the purchase.

[ ] ROSOrin / complete AI robot kits, robot dogs, and arms
    These hide many of the exact motor, power, encoder, and bridge decisions your project ladder is meant to teach. Revisit after you have built and instrumented the base yourself.

[ ] Random bare DC motors without gear-ratio, stall-current, torque, and mounting data
    Cheap is not cheap if no matching wheel, bracket, encoder, or driver can be selected from a datasheet.

[ ] LiPo/Li-ion cells, custom packs, and generic chargers
    Delay until you can state cell count, chemistry, charge voltage, continuous/peak current, connector, fuse, low-voltage cutoff, and enclosure plan. Never charge an unknown pack unattended.

IN-STORE QUESTIONS TO ASK / CHECK WITH YOUR PHONE
=================================================

For every motor:
[ ] Nominal voltage
[ ] No-load current and speed
[ ] Stall current
[ ] Gear ratio and torque
[ ] Encoder type, voltage, counts per motor revolution, and counts per wheel revolution
[ ] Shaft dimensions, matching wheels, and brackets
[ ] Datasheet/manual URL, not just packaging claims

For every motor driver:
[ ] Motor-voltage range
[ ] Continuous current PER CHANNEL with realistic cooling
[ ] Peak-current duration
[ ] Logic-high compatibility with 3.3 V
[ ] Brake/coast behavior
[ ] Current limiting, fault, and thermal protection
[ ] Enough headroom over measured motor stall current

For chassis/kits:
[ ] Differential drive, not mecanum, for the first mapping build
[ ] Rigid encoder mounts and non-slipping wheel hubs
[ ] Space for battery, switch, fuse, controller, driver, wiring, and later Pi/LiDAR
[ ] M2/M3 or clearly documented mounting pattern
[ ] Easy battery access and a reachable physical power switch
[ ] Slow enough for safe indoor tests

For batteries/power:
[ ] Exact chemistry and cell count
[ ] Correct matching charger
[ ] Continuous and peak current capability
[ ] Fuse and main-switch plan
[ ] Separate motor and logic regulation with an intentional shared ground
[ ] No custom multi-cell lithium pack as a beginner shortcut

For LiDAR/cameras:
[ ] Linux ARM64 driver and ROS 2 package for the intended distribution
[ ] Included data/power cable
[ ] Power draw and connector
[ ] Minimum and maximum range, scan rate, and indoor-light limitations
[ ] Mount dimensions or downloadable CAD
[ ] Return policy in case driver support is not as advertised
